While I remember listening to some songs from Geese's _3D country_ in 2024, mostly passively at the time sadly, it was really the beginning of 2025 that I started paying attention to their frontman Cameron Winter. As I have a perfectly tailored TikTok algorithm, videos using a sound snippet from _Love Takes Miles_ started appearing right around the time it was released, and as is typical for me, I took my sweet time and only really listened to the album's hit single a handful of times through Spotify recommendations, never searching for it or looking it up. According to LastFM, I listened to it exactly 8 times from January to June, before completely stopping until October. 

<Track id="2zf1izCOz2F22PF27uhxRF" title="Love Takes Miles" artist="Cameron Winter" label="music" />

Now, October of 2025 was around the time I moved back in with my parents from my university housing in Luxembourg. I still had one semester ahead of me and spent it mostly in front of my PC coding, in my parents' kitchen cooking, and meeting old friends from my high school years. I also had the misfortune of reinjuring a cracked disc in my lower back, which sadly made me stop from visiting the gym until February of the next year. (I was able to look this up in my fitness application that I use to enter the gym, and checking the monthly entries, it's incredible how much my life I track on apps out there). As such, day-to-day life at the time proved to be repetitive and at times dull. Now my first entry shall not be a discussion of my past year, but I believe that this backdrop serves to show my deep appreciation for Cameron Winter's work. 

Specifically, it was the earlier-mentioned and familiar _Love Takes Miles_ that suddenly sounded new and full of emotion. I remember the[ music video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U4UFAejSQFA) to the song also being released around November and being taken aback at how much it resonated with me. 

As such, my interest was piqued, and I started to dive deeper into Cameron Winter's only released album at the time of writing, called Heavy Metal. Now, contrary to the title, there is no heavy metal on this album. Instead, what one finds is a young Cameron Winter with oftentimes just a piano or a guitar, singing in his unique voice and tone. 

For example on the track _Nina + Field of Cops_, an erratic Cameron Winter sings about his love for Nina Simone and his struggles as an artist to create music. At least that is how I interpret this song, as you might know, interpreting his music can be done in various ways. Now, this song is the third-to-last on the album, but I want to continue the thread of which song I became obsessed over at what period in my life. It was during my visit in Stockholm in late spring/early summer, that I became addicted to this songs uplifting chorus and chaotic verses. I remember specifically sitting on the train from Marsda to Philips apartment and finding on each relisten another nugget of a lyric that I had previously missed.

<Track id="78ugJH8q6W3kiGLwM2K7Lg" title="Nina + Field of Cops" artist="Cameron Winter" label="music" />

The next song that followed this obsession was Try as I may, which became the soundtrack for my family visit to Nice this summer. We spent my sister's birthday there, and I spent a good chunk reading Norwegian Wood by Murakami. I specifically remember walking to a bakery the morning of my sister's birthday to grab breakfast for everyone. Now I tell you what, there was not a sadder song that I could've listened to on that beautiful morning walking on the Cote d'azure. Try As I May, I believe, is one of the more straightforward songs on the album, where Cameron expresses that despite your best efforts, you cannot force love to work. I did not feel heartbroken nor was I in a one-sided relationship at the time, but I felt some of the poignant lyrics on that track however they fit. I think that's something that this album, and Cameron in general, does incredibly well. The lyrics may fit an underlying narrative, but they're also nonsensical and visual, such that anyone can picture the imagery laid in front of them. This song in particular has some of my favourite lyrics of the album. 

<Track id="60WGJKqscT0bya3hPeQdYv" title="Try As I May" artist="Cameron Winter" label="music" />

....

The thought entered my head to rank every song on this album, but I feel similarly to Blonde by Frank Ocean, where I have listened to the whole album front to back so many times that every track feels essential and part of the whole. Interpret that as you will; maybe that means that every track is a 10.

This album has truly shaped my last year in many ways, and I am incredibly grateful for having another piece of music that, in a few years' time upon relistening, will transport me back to 2026 with all of its ups and downs.
